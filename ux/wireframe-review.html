Wireframes for the flight session

Six low-fidelity screens in the order a player meets them on a flight. Greys and boxes only: layout, hierarchy and wording are what's up for review. Art paints over these once the Director approves them.

  • Glance testEvery decision screen reads in 5 seconds; at most three numbers per card.
  • Never colour aloneQuality uses pips plus a word; warnings and affixes carry words and icons.
  • Click budget"Same as last pull", then "Pull": two clicks from prep to fight.
  • Interruption-safeLanding pauses or finishes; nothing pending is ever lost.

1. Takeoff

The flight starts. A small toast offers the board; it never opens by itself.

  1. 1Never opens the board by itself: the player chooses, so a flight is never hijacked.
  2. 2Two actions only. Also reachable by the minimap button or /fpr. Auto-hides after 8 seconds and never stacks.

2. Hub

One screen with everything on offer, and one obvious next step.

  1. 1This week's two affixes always visible, each with an icon and a name, never colour alone.
  2. 2One primary action: what most players want next. One click from takeoff to prep.
  3. 3Everything else in three plain columns. Progress as text (H 4/5), not just bars.
  4. 4Problems surface on the hub (low morale, benched characters) so they are not discovered mid-prep.

3. Prep

The main decision screen. Built so a 2-minute flight is enough.

  1. 1One line on what the boss does and what it tests, plus a warning chip in words. The chip never relies on colour.
  2. 2Four zones in fight order. Each card shows at most three numbers: gear level, power, morale. Quality is pips, not colour.
  3. 3Bench and benched characters stay visible; dashed outline means unavailable until reset.
  4. 4Tactic cards show their effect on the card itself, so nobody needs a tooltip to choose.
  5. 5Click budget: 'Same as last pull' then 'Pull' is two clicks. The Pull button is the largest target on the screen.

4. Pull

The fight is already decided; playback tells the story.

  1. 1Round counter is the biggest number: it is also the Efficient Kill score, so players learn it.
  2. 2Raid frames show health as bars with names; death is written out, not just a grey bar. Healers also show mana.
  3. 3The log tells the story in plain sentences, one per key moment. This is where the jokes land.
  4. 4Motion settings: pause, double speed, skip, or summary only (no animation at all).

5. Results

Outcome first, then loot, morale and any event choice.

  1. 1Outcome first, in words and a solid banner: kill or wipe, round, score change.
  2. 2Loot comes with a suggested recipient; the player can accept in one click or change it. Quality shown as pips plus the word.
  3. 3Morale and benchings listed in plain sentences, with the raid leader's note.
  4. 4Events are a card with exactly two choices and their costs written on the buttons. A pending choice survives landing.

6. Landing

Landing mid-activity never costs anything.

  1. 1Two equal-weight choices, both safe. The copy says what happens, not what the system does.
  2. 2A visible countdown, so an alt-tabbed player never comes back to a frozen screen. Default is Pause.

Decisions for the Director

  1. Board size: 680 by 440 pixels at UI scale 1, so the flight map stays partly visible. Bigger and roomier, or smaller and more compact?
  2. Loot suggestions: results pre-fill a suggested recipient (best upgrade), accepted in one click. Keep, or always choose by hand?
  3. Default playback: animated at normal speed, or summary-only by default on flights shorter than 3 minutes?
  4. Approval gate: approve these six, and Art starts the notebook style on top of them.
