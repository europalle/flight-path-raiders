Phase 2 · Deliverable 2a · Mid-fidelity · UX/UI & Accessibility · 2026-11-18

Wireframes for the flight session

Mid-fidelity, step 2 of 4. Twelve screens on the wide board: a 960 x 540 design grid that scales to about 70% of screen width by default (70 to 120% in Settings). Real text sizes and real content; still greyscale, since look and feel is Art's next step. Low-fi versions are kept in ux/wireframes/low-fi.

Ochre symbols are icon textures, not text characters: WoW's in-game fonts can't draw them, so Art supplies each one as a small texture.

  • Glance testEvery decision screen reads in 5 seconds; at most three numbers per card.
  • Never colour aloneQuality uses pips plus a word; warnings and affixes carry words and icons.
  • Click budget"Same as last pull", then "Pull": two clicks from prep to fight.
  • Interruption-safeLanding pauses or finishes; nothing pending is ever lost.

1. Takeoff

The flight starts. A small toast offers the board; it never opens by itself.

  1. 1Same two actions as low-fi, now at real text sizes (16 title, 12 body). Opens nothing by itself; /fpr and the minimap button also work.

2. Hub

One screen with everything on offer, and one obvious next step.

  1. 1Fixed navigation row on every top-level screen: Hub, Roster, Tavern, Guild Hall, Bank. Settings stays on the gear icon in the title bar. The bank tab shows a lock until it's unlocked.
  2. 2The Continue card grows to 152 units with a 210 x 128 boss illustration (Art's request). This week's affixes moved inside it, next to the fight they affect.
  3. 3Columns become one-line rows (name left, status right) to make room. Progress stays as text.
  4. 4Problems say who and why, not just a count.

2b. Roster

Specs, respec and gear live here.

  1. 1Table, not cards: 10 rows visible, sortable and filterable by role. Benched rows are dashed; morale under 40 gets a warning icon plus the number.
  2. 2Detail panel answers 'who is this?' in one glance: class, level, gear level, trait.
  3. 3Spec is chosen here and only here. Three buttons with role underneath; the passive is written out in full below.
  4. 4Respec shows the price in play time and the reason it rose. A Tome of Respec is always offered first when you have one.

2c. Tavern

Hiring recruits.

  1. 1Reroll price is on the button and doubles each reroll that day, so the cost is never a surprise.
  2. 2Five cards, each answers: who, what class and spec, how rare (pips plus word), how strong, and the trait that makes them funny.
  3. 3Warlocks are a 5% find (the guild has none), so they get a tag in words when one shows up.
  4. 4Full roster is explained before the player clicks Hire, with both ways out.

2d. Guild Hall

DKP upgrades, including the bank.

  1. 1One card per building: what it does, its level, and the price on the button. The only button you can afford right now is the dark one, and the card's border says so too.
  2. 2Locked buildings list their requirement in words. The Guild Bank costs DKP like every other building (Tuning: 150 DKP); only its second tab costs gold.

3. Prep

The main decision screen. Built so a 2-minute flight is enough.

  1. 1Boss brief now names what the fight tests and how this week's affix touches it.
  2. 2Cards are 156 x 58 with quality pips; four zones in fight order, never more than four cards per row.
  3. 3Bench column is fixed width so drag targets stay big at 70% scale.
  4. 4Selected tactic gets a tick as well as the dark fill, so it reads without colour.
  5. 5Pull is the largest target (180 x 50). Prices match the tuned economy.

4. Pull

The fight is already decided; playback tells the story.

  1. 1The wide board buys a 620 x 330 stage: raid formation left, boss right, floor line for Art to animate on. Boss wind-ups get a '!' marker plus a text label, not just an animation.
  2. 2Side column keeps the round counter as the biggest number, with the Efficient Kill target beside it.
  3. 3Log stays in plain sentences, five lines visible, newest in bold.
  4. 4Raid frames move to a bottom strip of 10, so names never truncate. Death is written as DEAD; healers show mana.
  5. 5Motion setting is visible here and lives in Settings. Reduced motion keeps the stage still and only updates bars.

5. Results

Outcome first, then loot, morale and any event choice.

  1. 1Outcome banner adds the guild rank change, since that is what people talk about in guild chat.
  2. 2Loot shows armor type, set piece and binding (BoP or BoE). A BoE nobody can use pre-fills 'Bank' with free slots; a BoP nobody can use pre-fills 'Sell'. Every row is a dropdown.
  3. 3Raid changes include the quit warning, so it never comes as a surprise.
  4. 4Drama card unchanged in logic: two choices, costs written on the buttons.

6. Landing

Landing mid-activity never costs anything.

  1. 1Same logic as low-fi at real sizes. Countdown defaults to Pause, so an alt-tabbed player never returns to a frozen screen.

7. Guild Bank

End-game storage: 16 slots, gear and consumables compete.

  1. 1Mailbox holds at most 5 overflow items. Timers count days you play, scaled by quality (Common 3, Uncommon 5, Rare 7, Epic 10), with a 30-calendar-day cap. Beyond 5, overflow sells at once.
  2. 2Tabs can be renamed (local only). Tuning: unlock at roster average 60 plus a first Normal raid kill, about 2 h of play; Tab 2 adds 16 slots for about 6 h; 32 is the cap. Poor junk auto-sells.
  3. 3Every gear slot shows an upgrade marker: who it helps and by how much, or 'no upgrade'. No special label for cheap gear; the names carry the joke. Consumables stack 5 per slot.
  4. 4Detail panel shows required level, who it fits and one-click equip. Equipping binds it.
  5. 5Quest items never use slots. 'Sell no-upgrade' clears everything nobody can use in one click.

7b. Guild Bank (locked)

Before level 60 and a first raid kill.

  1. 1Locked state lists every requirement as a checklist with your current value, so the goal is clear before the bank exists.

8. Settings

New: board scale and position, motion, colour-blind palettes, text size.

  1. 1Five tabs on the left; the mock shows Display with previews of Playback and Accessibility so you can judge the full set in one view.
  2. 2Scale slider 70 to 120%. Default is about 70% of screen width at 16:9, matching the world map size you approved. Position unlocks for dragging and is saved per character; /fpr reset is the escape hatch.
  3. 3Motion: Full (default), Reduced (stage holds still, bars and log update) or Summary only (straight to results).
  4. 4Colour-blind palettes and a text size bump. Every state is already shape plus text, so palettes are a comfort, not a crutch.
